This page covers the foundational agreements that shape how the team works — from async-first communication to how decisions get made. These aren't rules handed down from above; they're shared commitments the team has chosen to hold each other to. Read them, internalise them, and flag it if you see us falling short.

## Core Principles

These four principles underpin everything else on this page. When you're unsure how to handle a situation, come back to these.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Default to Async" icon="comments">
    Write things down before scheduling a meeting. If a message, doc, or comment can move something forward, use that first. Protect each other's deep work time.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Default to Transparency" icon="eye">
    Share context broadly and use private channels sparingly. When information is visible to everyone, better decisions get made and nobody is left in the dark.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Bias to Action" icon="person-running">
    Make reversible decisions quickly and learn from them. Only escalate when a decision is hard to undo. Waiting for perfect information is usually more costly than moving and adjusting.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Assume Good Intent" icon="handshake">
    Interpret ambiguous messages charitably. If something feels off, ask a clarifying question before reacting. Most misunderstandings are about words, not intent.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Working Agreements

These are the day-to-day norms the team has agreed to follow. They apply across all functions unless a team has explicitly documented a local exception in their playbook.

- **Core hours** are 10am–4pm in your local timezone. Aim to be available for collaboration and live conversations during this window.
- **Meeting-free blocks** run every Tuesday and Thursday morning. Protect this time for deep work and avoid scheduling internal meetings during these hours.
- **Async response time** is within 24 hours on normal working days. You are not expected to reply to messages outside of your working hours.
- **Urgent matters** should be flagged by prefixing your message with `[URGENT]` and using the `@here` mention only in the designated urgent-comms channel. Reserve this for genuine blockers or incidents.
- **Status updates** — if you're out, heads-down, or otherwise unavailable during core hours, set your Slack status so the team knows.

## Decision-Making

Most decisions are made by the **directly responsible individual (DRI)** — the single person accountable for a given area or project. The DRI consults others as needed, but ultimately makes the call and owns the outcome. This keeps us moving without requiring consensus on everything.

For a full breakdown of how we categorise decisions, who gets consulted versus who gets informed, and what to do when there's genuine disagreement, see the [Decision-Making Framework](/processes/decision-making).

## Feedback Culture

We give feedback that is **direct, kind, and specific**. Direct means you say the thing clearly rather than hinting at it. Kind means you assume the other person wants to improve and you care about helping them. Specific means you describe a behaviour and its impact, not a character judgment.

Feedback flows in all directions — peer to peer, upward, and downward — and shouldn't be saved for formal reviews. That said, for the structured review cycle, promotion criteria, and how growth conversations work, see [Performance & Growth](/people/performance).
