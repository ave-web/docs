This page describes how the company is organized — the key functions, how they relate to each other, and where to find the right people. Whether you're new and getting oriented or you're a veteran trying to loop in the right stakeholders, use this as your reference for navigating the org.

## Functions

We're organized into five core functions. Each owns a distinct domain, but they work together constantly. Click into each function to see what it covers and who to reach out to.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Engineering">
    **What they own:** Product infrastructure, backend and frontend development, data engineering, security, and technical operations. Engineering teams are organized around product areas and platform layers.

    **Who to contact:** Reach out to your engineering lead for team-specific questions, or post in `#team-engineering` for general inquiries. For incidents and on-call coverage, check the on-call rotation schedule shared by your engineering lead.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Product & Design">
    **What they own:** Product strategy, roadmap prioritization, feature definition, user research, and design systems. Product and Design work as an integrated function embedded across engineering teams.

    **Who to contact:** Post in `#team-product` for roadmap questions or to share customer feedback. For design reviews or asset requests, reach out in `#team-design` or file a request in Linear.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Go-to-Market (Sales, Marketing & CS)">
    **What they own:** Revenue generation, brand and demand generation, customer acquisition, account management, and post-sale success. GTM includes Sales, Marketing, and Customer Success as a unified revenue org.

    **Who to contact:** For sales inquiries, contact your regional sales lead. For marketing campaigns or press, reach out in `#team-marketing`. Customer escalations go to `#team-cs`.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Finance & Operations">
    **What they own:** Financial planning and analysis, budgeting, legal and compliance, vendor management, procurement, and office/IT operations. Finance and Ops keeps the company running smoothly behind the scenes.

    **Who to contact:** For budget questions or expenses, post in `#team-finance`. For IT, tools access, or equipment, use `#help-it`. Legal questions should go through your manager first.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="People & Culture">
    **What they own:** Recruiting, onboarding, learning and development, compensation and benefits, performance management, and employee experience. People & Culture supports every team across the full employee lifecycle.

    **Who to contact:** For onboarding questions, benefits, or HR matters, reach out in `#help-people` or contact your People Business Partner directly.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Working Across Teams

Most meaningful work happens across team boundaries. When kicking off a cross-functional project, establish a clear DRI (Directly Responsible Individual) on day one — this is the person who owns the outcome, not just their slice of the work. Project leads coordinate timelines and decisions, but the DRI is accountable.

When you need to loop in another function, start with a Slack message to their team channel before scheduling a meeting. Async-first keeps everyone's calendars lighter and creates a paper trail that's useful later. If a project is large enough to span multiple sprints or quarters, consider creating a shared project brief in Notion so everyone is working from the same context.

## Finding People

When you need to track down the right person or team, use these resources:

- **Company directory** — Search by name, role, or team at [directory placeholder]. Every employee profile includes their function, manager, and Slack handle.
- **Slack channels** — Browse the channel list filtered by `#team-` to find each function's primary channel. Most teams also have sub-channels for specific projects.
- **Team pages in your people tool** — Your HR or people platform contains org charts, reporting lines, and team rosters. Use this to understand structure and find stakeholders quickly.
- **Ask your manager** — If you're not sure who owns something, your manager is your fastest path to the right person.

<Tip>
  When you join a project that crosses team lines, make sure there's a clear DRI. Ambiguity is the enemy of speed.
</Tip>
