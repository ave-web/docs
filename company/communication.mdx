Good communication means putting the right information in the right place so anyone can find it when they need it. It's not about talking more — it's about talking smarter. These guidelines exist to reduce noise, protect deep work time, and make sure nothing important falls through the cracks.

## Tools We Use

Each tool has a job. Use the right tool for the right purpose, and you'll save yourself and your teammates a lot of time.

| Tool | Used For | Response Time |
|------|----------|---------------|
| **Slack** | Real-time chat, team channels, quick questions | Within 4 hours (during working hours) |
| **Email** | External communications, formal notices, vendor correspondence | Within 24 hours |
| **Video Call** (Zoom / Meet) | Synchronous discussion, 1:1s, team meetings, complex problem-solving | Scheduled in advance |
| **Notion / Docs** | Async documentation, project specs, meeting notes, proposals | Asynchronous — no expected response time |
| **Company Brain** | Policies, playbooks, processes, onboarding reference | Reference only — not a communication channel |

## Slack Norms

Slack is our real-time communication layer, but it can quickly become a source of distraction if we're not intentional about how we use it. Follow these norms to keep channels useful for everyone.

- **Use threads for replies.** When responding to a message in a channel, reply in-thread rather than in the main channel. This keeps conversations grouped and the channel feed scannable.
- **Use `@here` and `@channel` sparingly.** These tags notify everyone in the channel — only use them when the message is truly urgent and relevant to every member. For most updates, just post without a mention.
- **Follow channel naming conventions.** We use prefixes to make channels easy to find and understand at a glance:
  - `#team-` for function or team channels (e.g., `#team-engineering`)
  - `#proj-` for time-bound project channels (e.g., `#proj-q3-launch`)
  - `#announce-` for broadcast announcements (e.g., `#announce-company`)
  - `#social-` for non-work conversation (e.g., `#social-pets`)
- **DMs are fine for quick questions — but not for decisions.** If a conversation in a DM produces a decision, agreement, or action item, copy the outcome into a relevant shared channel or document. Decisions made in private are hard to reference later and create knowledge silos.

## Writing Things Down

We default to async communication because it respects people's time, creates a record, and lets teammates in different time zones participate equally. When you make a decision in a meeting, write it down immediately. When you start a project, create a brief. When a process changes, update this site.

Async-first doesn't mean slow — it means deliberate. The habit of writing things down compounds over time into an organization that can move fast without constant syncing. Learn more about our async principles at [How We Work](/how-we-work).

## Escalation Path

If you need a response and aren't getting one, follow this path before escalating further.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Post in the relevant channel">
    Start by posting your question or request in the appropriate team or project channel. This gives the right people visibility and keeps a record of the ask.
  </Step>
  <Step title="DM the person directly">
    If you haven't heard back within the expected response window, send a direct message to the person or their manager. Reference your original channel post so they have context.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use @urgent or call for true emergencies">
    For genuine time-sensitive emergencies — production incidents, safety issues, or deal-critical situations — use the `@urgent` tag in the relevant channel or reach the person by phone. Reserve this for situations where delay causes real harm.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Avoid making decisions in DMs. Copy the outcome into a shared channel or doc so the team has context.
</Warning>
