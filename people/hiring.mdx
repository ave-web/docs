Hiring is one of the most important things we do. This page explains how we approach it — fairly, quickly, and with a high bar.

## Hiring Philosophy

We hire for mission alignment, strong fundamentals, and growth trajectory. We care more about how someone thinks and learns than whether their resume checks every box. We actively seek out diverse backgrounds and perspectives because we believe they make our teams stronger and our products better. Every hire raises or lowers the bar — so we take the process seriously and we expect everyone involved to do the same.

## Interview Process

Every candidate goes through a consistent process. The specific steps may vary slightly by role, but the overall structure stays the same to keep things fair and efficient.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Recruiter screen (30 min)">
    The recruiter connects with the candidate to cover role expectations, compensation range, timeline, and mutual fit. This is also a chance for the candidate to ask early questions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Hiring manager interview (45 min)">
    The hiring manager dives deeper into the candidate's experience, motivation, and alignment with the team's needs. This is a two-way conversation about the role and the opportunity.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Technical or skills assessment (varies by role)">
    Depending on the role, candidates complete a take-home assignment, a live coding exercise, a portfolio review, or a case study. The format is communicated clearly in advance.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Team interviews (2–3 conversations)">
    Candidates meet with members of the team across different functions. Each interviewer focuses on a specific competency area defined in the scorecard.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reference checks">
    The recruiter or hiring manager contacts 2–3 professional references provided by the candidate. References are checked before an offer is extended.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Offer">
    The recruiter extends a verbal offer, followed by a written offer letter. Once accepted, the recruiting coordinator kicks off the onboarding process.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Interview Principles

These principles guide how everyone on an interview panel should show up.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Structured Interviews" icon="scale-balanced">
    Ask every candidate for the same role the same core questions. Consistency makes evaluation fair and your data more reliable when comparing candidates.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Rubric-Based Scoring" icon="list-check">
    Evaluate candidates against clearly defined criteria — not gut feel. Submit your scorecard in the ATS immediately after the interview while your memory is fresh.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Avoid Bias" icon="ban">
    Do not ask about age, family status, religion, national origin, or anything unrelated to the candidate's ability to do the job. If you're unsure whether a question is appropriate, don't ask it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Respect Their Time" icon="clock">
    Give candidates timely feedback at every stage. Don't leave people waiting for weeks with no update. Even a rejection deserves a prompt, respectful response.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## How to Refer Someone

Employee referrals are one of our best sources of great hires. If you know someone who'd be a strong fit for an open role, here's how to refer them.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check open roles in the ATS">
    Browse the current openings at [ATS link placeholder] to find a role that matches the person you have in mind.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit their resume and a note">
    Use the referral portal to submit their resume along with a brief note explaining why you think they'd be a great fit and how you know them.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Track their progress">
    You'll receive notifications as your referral moves through the interview stages. The recruiter may also reach out to you for additional context.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Receive your referral bonus">
    If your referral is hired and remains with the company for 6 months, you'll receive a referral bonus. See the current bonus amount in the HR portal.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Joining an Interview Panel

If you want to get involved in hiring, reach out directly to the recruiter for any open role. You don't need a formal invitation — we welcome employees who are enthusiastic about helping evaluate candidates. Before your first interview, you'll receive the scorecard, the interview guide, and a brief on what competency area you're covering. If it's your first time interviewing, ask the recruiter to pair you with an experienced interviewer for your first panel.

<Note>
  Candidate information is confidential. Don't discuss candidates outside the interview process or with people not on the panel.
</Note>
