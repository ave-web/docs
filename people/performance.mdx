## How Reviews Work

Reviews involve input from multiple directions — yourself, your peers, and your manager. Here's how the process flows from start to finish.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Self-review">
    Write a reflection covering your goals from the last cycle, the impact you had, what you're proud of, and the areas where you want to grow. Be honest and specific — this is your chance to shape the narrative around your work.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Peer feedback">
    Request feedback from 2–3 peers who have worked closely with you. Choose people who can speak to different aspects of your work. Their responses are shared with your manager to inform the review.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Manager review">
    Your manager writes their assessment of your performance, drawing on your self-review, peer feedback, and their own observations. This covers output, behavior, and growth against your level expectations.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Calibration">
    Managers meet as a group to align on ratings and ensure consistency across teams. This step helps prevent bias and ensures similar performance is recognized similarly across the company.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Conversation">
    Your manager shares the feedback in a dedicated 1:1. This is a two-way conversation — come prepared with questions and your own perspective. Compensation changes, if any, are communicated at this stage.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Goals">
    Together with your manager, set clear goals for the next review cycle. Goals should be ambitious but achievable, and tied to both team priorities and your personal growth.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Goal-Setting (OKRs)

We use OKRs (Objectives and Key Results) to align work from the company level down to individuals. Every quarter, the leadership team sets company-level OKRs. Teams then write their own OKRs that support those company objectives. From there, you work with your manager to define your individual OKRs that connect to your team's priorities.

Good OKRs are specific, measurable, and time-bound. The objective describes what you want to achieve; the key results define how you'll know you got there. Aim for 3–5 key results per objective. You can find templates and examples in [OKR resource link placeholder].

We have a defined leveling framework for both individual contributor (IC) and management tracks. Each level describes the expected scope, impact, and behaviors at that stage of your career. Levels are used to calibrate compensation, set growth expectations, and guide performance conversations.

## Feedback Culture

We believe feedback should be direct, specific, and kind. You don't need to wait for review season — share feedback with teammates whenever it's relevant. When you give feedback, focus on observable behavior and concrete impact rather than personality. When you receive it, listen openly before responding.

For more on how we communicate and collaborate day-to-day, see [How We Work](/how-we-work).

<Tip>
  The best performance reviews have no surprises. Give and receive feedback continuously, not just during review season.
</Tip>
