Customer Success is responsible for ensuring customers achieve their desired outcomes with our product. This playbook covers the full customer lifecycle from kickoff to renewal — what to do at each stage, how to spot risk early, and how to run a renewal process that protects and grows revenue.

## Customer Lifecycle Stages

<Steps>
  <Step title="Kickoff">
    Schedule a welcome call within 3 days of close. Introduce the customer to their CSM, confirm the success criteria agreed during the sales process, and align on the onboarding plan and timeline.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Onboarding">
    Execute the 30-60-90 day plan. Guide the customer through product setup, integrations, and initial training. Confirm the core team is up and running by day 30.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Adoption">
    Monitor product usage against the benchmarks set at kickoff. Proactively reach out when engagement drops or key features aren't being used. Run check-in calls monthly at minimum.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Growth">
    Identify opportunities to expand usage — additional seats, new teams, or upgraded tiers. Introduce new features and product updates that align with the customer's goals. Flag expansion opportunities to Sales.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Renewal">
    Start the renewal conversation 90 days before the contract end date. Confirm ROI, address any outstanding issues, and agree on the value delivered. Never let a renewal surprise you or the customer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Advocacy">
    Turn happy customers into active advocates. Ask for case studies, reference calls, G2 reviews, or speaking opportunities. Advocates are your best pipeline source.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Health Scoring

Customer health scores give the team a quick, consistent view of account risk and engagement. Scores are tracked in [CRM/tool placeholder] and updated automatically based on usage signals, support activity, and engagement data.

- **Green:** The customer is actively engaged, key features are in use, and sentiment is positive. Maintain regular cadence and look for growth opportunities.
- **Yellow:** Engagement is declining or success criteria are off track. Book a check-in call immediately and identify what's blocking progress.
- **Red:** The account is at risk — usage has dropped sharply, a key contact has churned, or there is an open complaint. A red score requires an escalation plan.

<Warning>
  A health score drop to red should trigger an escalation call within 48 hours. Don't wait for the customer to come to you.
</Warning>

## QBRs (Quarterly Business Reviews)

A QBR is your opportunity to demonstrate value, align on goals, and deepen the relationship with the economic buyer. Every account on a paid annual plan should receive a QBR. Cover the following in every session:

- Recap of product usage and key metrics over the quarter
- Progress against the success criteria defined at kickoff
- Preview of the product roadmap and how it connects to the customer's goals
- Structured feedback collection — what's working, what isn't, and what they need next
- Agreed goals and priorities for the coming quarter

## Escalation Process

When an account shows signs of risk, move fast. Speed matters more than perfection in an escalation.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Identify the risk">
    Spot the risk signal — whether it's a health score drop, a direct complaint, a missed milestone, or a sudden drop in usage. Document what you're seeing clearly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Log it in CRM">
    Create a risk record in the CRM with a category (for example: adoption, executive sponsor change, budget, product gap) and a brief summary of the situation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Loop in your manager">
    Notify your CS manager within 24 hours. Don't try to manage a red-flag situation alone. Share the risk record and your initial read on the cause.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build a joint action plan">
    Work with the customer to create a shared action plan with specific steps, owners, and deadlines. Both sides need to be committed for the plan to work.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Update weekly until resolved">
    Send a weekly update to your manager and track progress against the action plan in CRM. The escalation stays open until health returns to yellow or green.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Renewal Process

<Steps>
  <Step title="Set a renewal reminder">
    Create a reminder 90 days before the contract end date. This gives you enough time to run a proper renewal process without pressure.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Schedule a renewal review">
    Book a renewal review meeting with the champion and, ideally, the economic buyer. Make it a value conversation, not just a transactional ask.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prepare a ROI summary">
    Pull together usage data, key wins, and progress against the original success criteria. Make the case for renewal concrete and customer-specific.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send the renewal quote">
    Issue the renewal quote at least 60 days before the contract expires. This gives both sides time to work through procurement and legal without rushing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Loop in Sales when needed">
    For complex renewals involving multi-year terms, significant price increases, or expansion components, bring in your Sales counterpart early. Don't navigate commercial complexity alone.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  The best time to start the renewal conversation is at the kickoff call. Set success criteria from day one and track them — renewal becomes much easier when the customer can see exactly what they've achieved.
</Tip>
