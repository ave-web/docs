This playbook defines our sales process — the stages a deal moves through, how to qualify opportunities, and how to close consistently.

## Sales Stages

| Stage | Description | Exit Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| Prospecting | Identify target accounts | ICP fit confirmed |
| Outreach | First contact made | Positive response or meeting booked |
| Discovery | Understand pain and budget | BANT/MEDDIC criteria partially met |
| Demo | Product walkthrough | Champion identified, next steps agreed |
| Proposal | Send contract/SOW | Pricing accepted in principle |
| Negotiation | Legal and commercial review | Redlines resolved |
| Closed Won | Deal signed | PO received or contract executed |
| Closed Lost | Deal lost | Loss reason logged in CRM |

## Qualification Framework

Use MEDDIC to qualify every opportunity. MEDDIC stands for Metrics, Economic buyer, Decision criteria, Decision process, Identify pain, and Champion. Work through each element during discovery to assess whether a deal is real, winnable, and worth your time.

<Accordion title="Metrics">
  What measurable outcomes does the customer want to achieve? Ask: "How will you define success 6 months from now?" and "What does the business impact look like in numbers?"
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Economic Buyer">
  Who controls the budget and signs the contract? Ask: "Who else is involved in approving a purchase of this size?" Make sure you have access to this person before you invest heavily in the deal.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Decision Criteria">
  What factors matter most to the buying team? Ask: "What does your ideal solution look like?" and "How are you evaluating vendors?" Understand both technical and commercial requirements.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Decision Process">
  How does the customer make purchasing decisions? Ask: "Walk me through how you've bought software like this before." Understand the steps, timelines, and who has to sign off at each stage.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Identify Pain">
  What problem are they trying to solve, and what happens if they don't solve it? Ask: "What's the cost of staying with your current approach?" Pain needs to be real, urgent, and felt by the economic buyer.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Champion">
  Who inside the account is selling for you when you're not in the room? A champion has influence, access to power, and a personal stake in the outcome. If you don't have one, find one — or treat the deal as at risk.
</Accordion>

## CRM Hygiene

Accurate CRM data makes your pipeline trustworthy and your forecasts reliable. Follow these steps consistently.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Log every interaction">
    Record every customer-facing interaction in Salesforce within 24 hours. Include calls, emails, meetings, and key messages.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep stages accurate">
    Move deals to the correct stage as soon as exit criteria are met. Don't leave a deal in Discovery when it's clearly in Proposal.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set next step dates">
    Every open opportunity must have a next step and a concrete date. If there's no agreed next step, the deal is stalled.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Log loss reasons">
    When you close a deal as lost, select the correct loss reason and add a note. This is required, not optional — it drives product and sales strategy decisions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tag key contacts">
    Mark your champion and economic buyer on the account record. Other team members need to know who the key people are without having to ask you.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Handoff to Customer Success

After you close a deal, your job isn't done until the customer is in good hands. Within 5 business days of close, make a warm introduction to the assigned Customer Success Manager. Complete the deal brief template in Salesforce — this includes the customer's success criteria, key contacts, commercial terms, and any commitments made during the sales process. Align with the CSM on the first 30-day plan before the kickoff call. A smooth handoff sets the customer up for early wins and protects your commission on renewal.

<Tip>
  The best reps qualify out fast. A clear no today is better than a slow maybe for 3 months.
</Tip>
