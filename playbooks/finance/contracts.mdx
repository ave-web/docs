This page explains how contracts work at the company — who reviews them, how quickly you can expect a response, and what to do when you receive a contract from a vendor or customer. Never sign a contract before reading this page first.

## Types of Contracts We Handle

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Customer Agreements" icon="handshake">
    MSAs, order forms, and SOWs tied to customer deals. Reviewed by Sales and Legal together. The Sales rep owns the commercial terms; Legal owns the legal review.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Vendor Contracts" icon="file-contract">
    Agreements for SaaS tools, professional services, and other third-party vendors. Reviewed by Finance (for budget impact) and Legal (for terms and liability).
  </Card>
  <Card title="NDAs" icon="lock">
    Mutual or one-way non-disclosure agreements. Use the standard NDA template for most cases. Non-standard NDAs from counterparties go through the full legal review process.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Employment & Contractor" icon="building-columns">
    Offer letters, consulting agreements, and contractor SOWs. Managed by People Ops with Legal oversight. Do not use custom templates without People Ops approval.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Contract Request Process

<Steps>
  <Step title="Submit a contract request">
    Fill out the legal intake form (link placeholder) with the contract attached. Do this as early as possible — do not wait until you're under deadline pressure.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Provide context">
    In the intake form, include the business context, the deadline for execution, the name of the business owner, and any commercial terms that have already been agreed verbally.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Legal acknowledges">
    Legal will acknowledge your request within 1 business day and confirm the expected turnaround time based on complexity and current queue.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review is completed">
    Standard agreements are reviewed within 3–5 business days. Complex agreements — those involving unusual liability, large contract values, or novel terms — take 7–10 business days.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Legal returns with comments or approval">
    Legal will either approve the contract as-is or return it with tracked comments and suggested changes. Review the comments before sharing anything with the counterparty.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Do not sign until approved">
    Do not sign, counter-sign, or formally accept any contract until Legal explicitly approves it. This includes clicking "I agree" on vendor sign-up flows with annual commitments.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## NDAs

For most situations, use the company's standard NDA template (link placeholder). The standard template is pre-approved and can be sent to counterparties without a full legal review. If a counterparty sends you their own NDA or requests changes to our standard form, submit it through the legal intake process. Turnaround for NDA reviews is 2 business days. Do not mark up a counterparty NDA yourself — send it to Legal.

## Redlines and Negotiation

When a counterparty returns a contract with changes, follow this process to keep the negotiation on track without bypassing Legal.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Receive the redlined contract">
    Download the redlined version and review it at a high level to understand what the counterparty has changed. Don't agree to anything verbally yet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Forward to Legal with context">
    Send the redlined contract to Legal via the intake form or directly if your Legal contact has already been assigned. Include a summary of the deal and any commercial context that might affect how Legal responds.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Legal reviews and prepares counter-redlines">
    Legal will review the counterparty's changes and produce a counter-redlined version with suggested responses. They'll flag any changes that are non-negotiable from a legal risk perspective.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Align with the counterparty">
    The Sales rep or business owner shares Legal's counter-redlines with the counterparty and works through the outstanding issues. Keep Legal copied on material conversations.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Repeat until final form is agreed">
    Continue the review cycle until both parties are satisfied with the contract language. Don't rush this step — unclear contract terms cause expensive disputes later.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Execute via DocuSign">
    Once Legal confirms the final version is approved, send the contract for signature via DocuSign. Both parties sign electronically. Store the fully executed copy in the contract repository.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Signing Authority

Only authorised individuals may sign contracts on behalf of the company. Check the table below before asking anyone to sign.

| Contract Value | Who Can Sign |
|---|---|
| Under $10k | Department head |
| $10k–$50k | VP or above |
| Over $50k | CEO or CFO |

<Warning>
  Never sign a contract on behalf of the company without the required approval. This applies to vendor sign-ups with annual commitments, too — clicking "agree" on a billing page is a legally binding act.
</Warning>
