Whether you're leaving for a new opportunity or the company is restructuring, this guide ensures the transition is smooth for you and the team. Following this process protects everyone — it preserves institutional knowledge, ensures a clean handoff, and closes the chapter with respect on both sides.

## For the Departing Employee

<Steps>
  <Step title="Notify your manager">
    Inform your manager of your decision and agree on a last day. Give as much notice as your contract requires — more if you can.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm payroll and benefits details">
    Work with People Ops to confirm the date of your final paycheck, when your benefits coverage ends, and any outstanding reimbursements.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Transfer project ownership and documentation">
    Hand off all active projects to the appropriate teammates. Document your work thoroughly so nothing is lost after you leave.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Return company equipment">
    Coordinate with IT to arrange the return of your laptop, peripherals, and any other company-owned hardware.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete the IT offboarding form">
    Fill out the IT offboarding form to begin the access revocation process. This covers all tools, accounts, and systems.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Attend an exit interview">
    Participate in an optional exit interview with People Ops. Your candid feedback helps the company improve for everyone who comes after you.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## For the Manager

<Steps>
  <Step title="Notify HR immediately">
    As soon as you know someone is leaving, inform HR so they can begin the administrative offboarding process without delay.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a knowledge transfer plan">
    Work with the departing employee to identify everything that needs to be documented or handed off before their last day. Build a plan with clear deadlines.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Announce the departure appropriately">
    Share the news with your team in a timely, respectful way. Work with the employee on the timing and framing of the announcement.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Coordinate access revocation with IT">
    Confirm with IT the exact date and scope of access removal. Ensure it happens on the last day of employment — no sooner, no later.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Collect company equipment">
    Follow up with the departing employee to make sure all company hardware is returned to IT before or on their last day.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Update documentation and the org chart">
    After the employee departs, update team docs, the org chart, and any process pages that referenced their role or responsibilities.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Knowledge Transfer

The most important thing a departing employee can do is document what they know. Institutional knowledge that lives only in someone's head disappears the moment they leave. Use Company Brain to capture processes, decisions, context, and anything else that a future teammate would need to understand your work. Even rough notes are better than nothing — your manager can help you prioritize what to document in your final weeks.

## IT Offboarding Checklist

- Revoke access to Slack, Google Workspace, GitHub, Salesforce, and all other company tools and SaaS products.
- Recover the employee's laptop, monitor, and any other company hardware.
- Update 1Password vaults to remove the departing employee and rotate any shared credentials they had access to.
- Remove the employee from all internal distribution lists, aliases, and mailing groups.
- Disable or transfer ownership of any service accounts or integrations they owned.

<Warning>
  Access revocation happens on the last day of employment. IT will process the offboarding checklist on that date — not before, and not after.
</Warning>
